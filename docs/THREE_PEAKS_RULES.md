# Three Peaks — canonical rules

2026-10-23. Web beta, not part of the native released collection or Club House.

Rules checked against publisher [Solitaire City: How to Play Tri-Peaks](https://www.solitairecity.com/iPhone/Tri-Peaks.shtml) and its [face-down variant](https://www.solitairecity.com/iPhone/Tri-Peaks_Rules.shtml) on 2026-10-23. This implementation automatically reveals newly uncovered cards and uses no speed bonuses or scoring. It is an independent implementation; no source code or artwork was copied.

## 1. Deal and objective

One standard 52-card deck without jokers. Shuffle using a deterministic uint32 seed and Fisher–Yates (the implementation is the version 1 deal definition). Deal 28 tableau cards into three overlapping peaks: rows of 3, 6, 9 and 10. The other 24 form the stock; turn its first card into the waste immediately, leaving 23 draws. Clear all 28 tableau cards to win. Deals are random; solvability is not guaranteed.

## 2. Legal play

Only a tableau card with both covering cards removed is exposed. The ten bottom cards start exposed. Covered cards are face down. An exposed card may move onto the waste when its rank is one above or below the waste rank. Suit is irrelevant. **Ace wraps: K–A–2 is allowed in either direction.** Equal ranks do not match. The removed card becomes the new waste. A draw turns the next stock card into the waste and is allowed even if a tableau move exists. There is one stock pass, no recycling. A win ends immediately; otherwise the game ends when the stock is empty and no exposed tableau card matches.

## 3. Undo and controls

Undo is free and unlimited, including draws and the move that ended a game. It returns to the exact preceding position without refunding elapsed play time. A win counts once per attempt even after undo, resume and a second completion. Tap an exposed card or the stock. Keyboard: Tab and Enter/Space operate the same visible buttons; arrow keys move between exposed tableau cards, D draws and Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes. Held state-changing keys do not repeat. Input is disabled behind confirmation/result overlays. The header opens RestartDialog with Retry same board (same shuffled deal) and New Game (fresh seed). Either asks before replacing an unfinished deal; from a result both start directly. Retry begins a new attempt, resets the move history and counted-win marker, and increments games played. Home preserves the unfinished game.

## 4. Screens and lifecycle

Home, at most three Quick Rules steps, game and statistics. Home uses GameHomeHeader and the shared Web beta notice. First tutorial completion enters play or resumes an existing deal. Tutorial Back marks it read. The result states either that all cards were cleared or that no moves remain, shows cards left, and offers Undo, New Game, Home and optional ShareAction. Only a win shares `completed`; a stuck game shares `played`. ResultAdSlot sits outside the board. No game guide is linked until one exists.

The play clock is for local statistics only. It counts visible play while the game screen is active, with no React render per second. Background, Home, collection exit and unmount book only unbooked seconds and save. Listeners and timers are released on unmount. A shortcut may resume the sole unfinished deal after Quick Rules have been read.

## 5. Persistence

Version 1, game-owned `tp.saveGame`, `tp.stats`, `tp.flags`. One resume slot; no daily mode. The save stores seed and at most 51 actions, elapsed seconds, a counted-win marker and savedAt. Loading replays every action through legal rules and rejects impossible actions, invalid seeds/clocks, unsupported versions and already finished positions. Invalid data is not deleted by reads. Backup uses the same schemas. Saving failure never blocks play. Unlimited undo survives a reload because the move history is retained.

Statistics are games started, wins, fewest cards left in a finished attempt, and total active play seconds. Starting a new deal or retry increments games; resume does not. Undo never subtracts a win and cannot award a second win for that attempt. A better finish after undo may improve fewest remaining. Completing a same-deal retry is a separate attempt and may award another win.

## 6. Presentation and localization

Cards use rank plus suit symbols; red and black are supplementary. Face-down cards do not reveal rank through their accessible names, face artwork or suit-colour classes. Exposed cards announce position, rank, suit and whether they can move. Cards have a traditional 5:7 proportion, original SVG suit marks and a symmetric geometric back. A subtle framed tableau separates the peaks from the stock/waste/undo rail. Playable cards have an accent edge and dot, with a distinct two-tone keyboard focus ring (below). The layout uses CSS percentages; its ten-card row gives each card a 26px-wide target at a 320px viewport. A scrolling play area keeps controls reachable on short screens. English is source. Japanese and the other 12 translations are AI-written drafts; neither author/native review nor independent back-translation has occurred. Numeric/card-rule literals and placeholders are consistent across 14 catalogs. No high-risk shared policy keys are added.

Look, 2026-10-29. The table is the title's accent tint (`--accent-soft`) lit from above by a lamp and set into the page with a soft recess (`--tp-lamp`, `--tp-well`). A card is paper with a hairline edge and a soft contact shadow (`--tp-lift`), the same recipe as Solitaire and Hearts, because the card/table difference alone is only 1.39:1 in the light theme and the edge line 1.02:1 — the shadow, not the line, parts a card from the table and from the card it overlaps by a pixel or two. Under a fine pointer a playable card lifts 2px with a larger shadow (`--tp-lift-raised`, 120ms ease-out); on any input a pressed playable card sinks 1px to a hairline (`--tp-press`). A face-down card is a real back: paper with a paper margin, and inside it the accent panel with the inner frame, weave and medallion it had before (the first `rect` of the back artwork carries `.tp-back-panel`); it still reveals nothing about the face. The stock is a pile — three paper edges stepped down and to the right plus a cast shadow — the waste has one edge, and an empty stock is the flat dashed recess it was, so "there is a pile / there is none" is told by the edge and not by colour (the count beside it is unchanged). The keyboard focus ring of a card is two-tone: a 3px band in the card ink (`--tp-ink`) filling the outline offset, then the 3px `--ink` outline. A row of cards overlaps by a pixel or two, so most of a focused card's ring lies on the paper of its neighbours, and in the dark theme `--ink` is paper-coloured (1.00:1) — the outline alone vanished there once the dark cards became paper. The band shows on the paper, the outline on the table; hover and press repeat the band in their own shadow. Undo's circle uses the shared control-key recipe (lit top edge, 2px lift, sinks when pressed, flat when disabled). The waste card settles for 140ms (it lands from 6px above, turned 1.5°, its shadow tightening) every time it changes — take, draw, Undo, new deal — but not when the screen opens. Reduced Motion, by the OS preference or the app setting, turns off the settle, the eased lift and the hover and press movement. Nothing is added to the board: no element, text or information, only thickness, contact shadow and recess.

Card colours are game content (docs/architecture/css-split.md) and are declared only in the two blocks at the top of `ui/three-peaks.css`; `ui/contrast.test.ts` measures them from the stylesheets and fails on any colour literal outside the blocks. **Owner's decision, 2026-10-29: cards are paper in both themes.** This reverses the earlier recorded decision that dark cards use the dark surface `#212730` with the red `#ef9ba1` (7.06:1), which put the cards below the table in brightness (1.26:1), turned the black suits near-white and made the untouchable face-down cards the brightest thing on the table (the other card games already keep paper in the dark theme). Tokens — light: `--tp-face` `#fffdf8`, `--tp-ink` `#232a33`, `--tp-face-line` `#ddd7ca`, `--tp-red` `#9f2634`; dark: `#eae6dd`, `#1d232b`, `#bfb9ac` and the same red, one value because it sits on paper in both themes. The dark paper is a little brighter than Solitaire's `#e6e2d9` so a playable card's 2px accent edge keeps 3:1 on it: `#b07373` on `#eae6dd` is 3.04:1 (2.93:1 on `#e6e2d9`). WCAG relative-luminance contrast, computed by `ui/contrast.test.ts` (the same formula as the other games' tests):

| Pair                                        | Light   | Dark    |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------- | ------- |
| Rank/suit ink on paper                      | 14.24:1 | 12.70:1 |
| Red suit on paper                           | 7.38:1  | 6.02:1  |
| Accent (playable edge, back panel) on paper | 8.99:1  | 3.04:1  |
| Focus ring band (`--tp-ink`) on paper       | 14.24:1 | 12.70:1 |
| Focus ring outline (`--ink`) on the table   | 10.26:1 | 9.57:1  |
| Focus ring outline (`--ink`) on paper       | 14.24:1 | 1.00:1  |
| Focus ring band against outline             | 1.00:1  | 12.69:1 |
| Paper on the table                          | 1.39:1  | 9.58:1  |
| Card edge on paper (decoration, no floor)   | 1.41:1  | 1.57:1  |

Text pairs are held to 4.5:1 and graphical objects to 3:1. The focus ring is held to 3:1 on each ground it can lie on: the band on paper and the outline on the table, both themes. The outline on paper is 1.00:1 in the dark theme, so there the band must part from the outline (12.69:1, gated); in the light theme both are the same dark ink and read as one thick ring, so the band-against-outline row is not gated there. The shadows, lamp and gloss carry no meaning and have no floor. The status label above the board, with the "/ 28" inside it, is `--ink` in both themes (`--ink` is 10.26:1 light and 9.57:1 dark on the table; `--ink-soft` was 4.09:1 light, before the lamp, and 3.93:1 dark, under the 4.5:1 a 12px label needs). This closes the light-theme gap that an earlier version of this section recorded. Card suit symbols also identify suits without colour. The title accent is owned by the shared brand palette.
